MAF FC Possession Explorer: three directions

Three structurally different answers to the same question: how do we present an H/A/D/X possession engine as both a hackathon showcase and a working analysis tool?

Every preview runs on the same data: the repository's synthetic end-to-end fixture (tests/test_game_state.py, 120 frames at 25 fps, graded half = frames 10–109). The Phase 3B and smooth50 labels shown are the real outputs of src/possession.py and src/baseline.py on that fixture. One shared playback clock drives every pitch, timeline and readout on this page.

Synthetic data only. No match data, predictions or ground truth are used or reconstructed. The fixture has 5 tracks, 4 of them parked at one spot, so the pitches look sparse on purpose. Keyboard: focus any preview, then Space plays or pauses, ←/→ step one frame, and Shift+arrow steps 10 frames.

More than a Hack 2027 · The Initial Challenge

Who has the ball, frame by frame, without guessing.

A deterministic, rule-based engine that labels every frame of a football match as home possession, away possession, dead ball, or no answer. No machine learning, no training data.

Watch it workHow it worksGitHub

Four labels, one per frame

Ranked on spellF1, which matches whole possessions by temporal overlap, so clean boundaries matter more than any single frame.

HHome possessionHome has sustained control, or kept it through a pass or a loose ball.
AAway possessionThe same, for the away side.
DDead ballBall out of play, before a set piece is struck, before kickoff.
XNo answerOutside a half, or before any possession can be established.

How it works

Five separate stages. Every parameter was fixed in advance from football reasoning.

  1. TrackingNearest home and away player to the ball, plus ball position and speed.
  2. Control evidenceWithin 2.0 m, with the other side at least 1.0 m further away.
  3. Dead ballBall out of play, the set-piece window before the strike, pre-kickoff.
  4. State machine10 control observations to switch, backdated; held through loose balls.
  5. OutputH, A or D inside the halves; X outside them.

Decision

  • If the app is mainly the team's research instrument, pick A.
  • If the app is mainly the judges' first impression, and the tool can be thin, pick B.
  • If one codebase must do both, and the extra pages are affordable, pick C. Build A's Explorer first (UI1), then the Overview page around it.
