# E1C-B - IDSSE alternative dataset feasibility

Status: documentation-only audit. No IDSSE match files were downloaded or processed, and no adapter, predictions, or scores were produced. This does not change the [SkillCorner E1C-A decision](SKILLCORNER_E1C_A.md).

## Rights and provenance

The [publisher paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04505-y) states that the DFL, the original collector, authorized publication of the **dataset** under CC BY 4.0 and that each of seven matches has three XML files: match information, events, and positions. The [authors' repository](https://github.com/spoho-datascience/idsse-data) repeats that the data are DFL-authorized CC BY 4.0 and requires credit to the Deutsche Fußball Liga (DFL) and citation of Bassek, Rein, Weber and Memmert (2025), DOI `10.1038/s41597-025-04505-y`. Thus the tracking, events and match metadata share the data grant; video is explicitly excluded from the release. The original dataset DOI is [10.6084/m9.figshare.28196177](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28196177). The Figshare landing page itself was inaccessible to this audit tool; verify its record/version and file hashes before downloading.

Under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode), local copying, private benchmarking, publication of aggregate results and derived labels, and redistribution of original or modified data are allowed, including commercial use, subject to attribution, a license link, indication of modifications, preservation of supplied notices, and no suggestion of DFL endorsement. This is a license-based permission, not a claim that every downstream score is accurate. If a derivative is shared, retain the DFL and paper attribution and identify the transformation. The [PySport Hugging Face mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/pysport/idsse-data) labels itself a mirror under CC BY 4.0; use the publisher grant as the rights source and verify mirror bytes/version against original Figshare before treating it as identical.

## File inventory and cost

Seven match IDs are `J03WMX`, `J03WN1`, `J03WOH`, `J03WOY`, `J03WPY`, `J03WQQ`, `J03WR9`. The original Figshare-backed [floodlight index](https://floodlight.readthedocs.io/en/develop/_modules/floodlight/io/datasets.html) and the mirror [file tree](https://huggingface.co/datasets/pysport/idsse-data/tree/main) agree on these IDs and three XML types per match: `DFL_02_01_matchinformation_*`, `DFL_03_02_events_raw_*`, and `DFL_04_03_positions_raw_observed_*`. The mirror card's listed `J03WPF` and `J03WQF` are inconsistent with its files; **do not use those two card IDs** without correction. The original publisher describes two Bundesliga 1 and five Bundesliga 2 matches, 1,002,644 position frames in total, and no licensed video.

The mirror advertises **2.63 GB total**. Its position XML files are about **349-419 MB per match**, events about **0.6-0.8 MB**, metadata about **12 kB**. A single complete match therefore needs roughly 0.35-0.42 GB download plus parsing and intermediate-array space; all seven need multi-GB storage. The published figures are mirror inventory estimates, not locally measured sizes. No complete file or bounded sample was downloaded in this phase. A future download should select one original-source match, record immutable source version, byte size and SHA-256, and keep it under ignored `external_samples/idsse/`.

## Tracking, event and label contract

The [paper's data record](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04505-y) describes 25 Hz TRACAB positions. XML `FrameSet` separates `GameSection` (first/second half), `TeamId` and `PersonId`; each `Frame` carries `N` (number), `T` (ISO-8601 local timestamp), `X/Y` (metres), `D` (cm since preceding frame), `S` (km/h), `A` (m/s^2), and `M` (minute). Ball frames additionally carry `Z`, `BallPossession` (1 home, 2 away) and `BallStatus` (0 inactive, 1 active). The paper defines possession by the player in control and inactive match state as referee-interrupted (for example foul or substitution). Metadata provides pitch size, team IDs and home/away designation, player IDs, and kickoff timing. The [DFL parser](https://floodlight.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/floodlight/io/dfl.html) derives period frame ranges from ball `FrameSet`s, maps players through teamsheets, and emits NaNs for absent player observations; it estimates frame rate from adjacent `T` values. Its [DFL pitch template](https://floodlight.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/floodlight/core/pitch.html) uses pitch-centred metre coordinates. Actual gap rates, ball missing-value codes, and code coverage have **not** been verified on full XML and must be measured before a benchmark.

The paper says events are human-operator annotated player, team and referee actions, each with an ISO-8601 timestamp. Team actions include kickoff, throw-in, corner and free kick; referee actions include start/final whistle and substitutions. The authors warn that events and tracking come from separate processes and their timestamps can differ. They did not apply an automatic synchronization correction. Treat event times as corroboration or a separately declared input, not an exact frame boundary or direct Smart Tagging equivalent. Verify time zone, clock offset and frame match before joining.

Proposed **provider-label** precedence, subject to actual XML audit:

| State | Required frame evidence | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H | Valid half/frame and `BallStatus=1`, `BallPossession=1` | Explicit provider annotation; not independent hand-labelled ground truth. |
| A | Valid half/frame and `BallStatus=1`, `BallPossession=2` | Explicit provider annotation. |
| D | Valid half/frame and `BallStatus=0` | Explicit provider inactive-match code; referee-interruption definition supports dead-ball evaluation once coverage and boundaries are checked. |
| X | Missing/invalid frame, missing/other codes, ambiguous transition or contradictory metadata | Unknown; never fill from an event gap. |

`BallStatus` is affirmative D evidence superior to SkillCorner phase gaps. Its detailed production/quality rules and code consistency remain to be audited. `BallPossession` is provided in the tracking file itself; the label channels must be stripped before prediction to avoid direct leakage. Event-only possession changes are timestamped, human-annotated **proxies** and subject to synchronization error; they are not a substitute for the explicit frame codes. Ball-out-of-play and restart events can corroborate status transitions, but a gap between events is not D.

## Frozen-engine feasibility

The frozen [Phase 3B engine](../src/possession.py) expects nearest home/away player distances to the ball, corner-origin ball `x/y`, ball speed in m/s, half segments, pitch dimensions, and optional Smart Tagging set-piece/kickoff windows. An IDSSE adapter would parse the three XMLs, validate `N/T/GameSection`, map `PersonId` through metadata to home/away, convert pitch-centred `X/Y` to corner-origin using half the metadata pitch dimensions, and retain raw `BallPossession/BallStatus` only in a separate label channel. `S` is in km/h and may be converted to m/s (`S/3.6`) only after its ball-field quality is checked; otherwise derive horizontal speed from genuinely adjacent observed `X/Y/T`, never across gaps or halves. Distances must ignore missing player/ball coordinates. Native **25 fps** matches the engine's original frame-count timing, so no resampling or threshold scaling is proposed; actual `T` cadence must confirm this per match.

DFL event names and times are not Smart Tagging windows. The first adapter should keep event tags absent and report the resulting restart limitation. A later event bridge needs explicit type mapping, time alignment and a missing-ball strike-gap test; do not alter the frozen engine or silently feed label/status codes into predictor features.

## Benchmark gate and protocol

**Conditional GO for a small IDSSE adapter**, beginning with synthetic contract tests and one complete licensed match after source-hash verification. The rights blocker that stopped SkillCorner does not apply to the publisher-authorized IDSSE release. The data-quality blockers remain: actual XML coverage and code values, frame continuity, ball/player missingness, event alignment, coordinate checks, and how `BallStatus` and `BallPossession` behave at stoppages and transitions. Until audited, no numeric agreement or official spell score is warranted.

For seven matches, predeclare **four development / three sealed test**: development `J03WMX`, `J03WOH`, `J03WOY`, `J03WPY`; test `J03WN1`, `J03WQQ`, `J03WR9`. This places one first-division match in each group, but the five second-division games share Fortuna Duesseldorf, so team-disjoint evaluation is impossible. Keep the frozen main algorithm as reference; evaluate all future methods on the same verified frames, labels and settings. Report per-match and paired aggregates with uncertainty, not generalized league accuracy. Do not tune on test matches or compare with competitor numbers from different match sets or definitions.

If complete, aligned `BallPossession/BallStatus` codes pass audit, frame-level H/A/D/X agreement, transition agreement and official-style spellF1 become feasible as **agreement with DFL provider codes**. If coverage is sparse or contradictory, restrict to coverage-limited agreement and publish coverage/exclusions; sparse X segments can fragment spells and make whole-match spellF1 misleading. No external model evaluation was run here.
