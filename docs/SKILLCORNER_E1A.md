## Verified schema and timing observations

- Metadata keys include `id`, `home_team`, `away_team`, `home_team_side`, `match_periods`, `pitch_length`, `pitch_width`, `players`, and `ball`. Home team ID is `1802`, away ID `866`; `home_team_side` is `['left_to_right', 'right_to_left']`. Pitch is **105 × 68 metres**. Player metadata `id` matches tracking `player_data[].player_id`; `trackable_object` does not. These mappings were checked on one frame.
- Tracking records have `frame`, `timestamp`, `period`, `ball_data`, `possession`, `player_data`, `image_corners_projection`. `ball_data` has `x,y,z,is_detected`; `possession` has `player_id,group`; `player_data[]` has `player_id,x,y,is_detected`. `group` values observed: `home team`, `away team`, null. Coordinates are pitch-centred metres per the [README](https://github.com/SkillCorner/opendata/blob/4340d274572876239c154c90bc507a9b3250a656/README.md), consistent with sampled ball positions. The tracking records have no observed ball-speed field.
- The initial 146 complete tracking records are contiguous frames **0–145**. Frames 0–9 have null `period` and `timestamp`, no players and null ball coordinates; frame 10 is period 1 at `00:00:00.00`, then timestamps advance by 0.10 s through frame 145. A separate 216-frame window at **28908–29123** is contiguous: period 1 through frame 28990, nine null-period frames 28991–28999, period 2 from frame 29000 at `00:45:00.00`. This verifies **10 fps in the sampled windows**; full-file continuity and sampling remain unaudited. Metadata `match_periods` lists `start_frame`/`end_frame` as 10/28990 and 29000/60300.
- In the initial window, 136 period-1 rows each contain 22 player records, but **1,040 of 2,992 player observations** have `is_detected=false` (extrapolated). Ball `is_detected` is true on 129 period-1 rows and false on seven; false does not mean coordinates are absent. During the halftime window, some in-period rows have null ball coordinates and `is_detected=null`. No full-match completeness rate can be inferred from these windows.
- The complete phase CSV has **423 rows** and fields including `match_id,frame_start,frame_end,time_start,time_end,period,attacking_side_id,team_in_possession_id,team_in_possession_shortname,team_in_possession_phase_type`. It has 206 period-1 and 217 period-2 rows. Assuming adjacent `frame_end == next frame_start` means half-open intervals, there are **47 positive gaps** within period 1 and **57** within period 2. The specification calls these associated start/end frames but does **not** state inclusivity; half-open interpretation needs provider confirmation.
- A period-1 phase is recorded at frames **28989–29032** with time `48:17.9–48:22.2`, while tracking switches to period 2 at frame 29000. Its period label cannot safely be broadcast over its full frame span. A period-2 phase starts at frame 29032. The actual tracking `period` must govern clipping, and the 28991–28999 hole must remain X.
- The first 130 complete rows of the dynamic-events prefix run from frame 29 to 1107. Observed fields include `event_type`, `frame_start`, `frame_end`, `time_start`, `time_end`, `period`, `team_id`, `game_interruption_before`, `game_interruption_after`, `start_type`, and `end_type`. Observed `event_type` values include `player_possession`, `passing_option`, `on_ball_engagement`, and `off_ball_run`. The interruption fields contain such values as throw-in, free-kick and goal-kick variants. The [provider specification](https://26560301.fs1.hubspotusercontent-eu1.net/hubfs/26560301/Guides/Dynamic%20Events/20250216%20-%20Dynamic%20Events%20CSV%20Specifications.pdf) says they describe an interruption before or after a player-possession event; they do not provide exact continuous dead-ball start/end frames.

## Label semantics and dead-ball decision

The [phase specification](https://26560301.fs1.hubspotusercontent-eu1.net/hubfs/26560301/Guides/Phases%20of%20Play/20250216%20-%20Phases%20of%20Play%20CSV%20Specifications.pdf) says phases are defined only while the ball is in play. It constructs team possession from player-possession events, holds a team between same-team events, changes team at the next team's event, and treats >30 seconds without player-possession, set-piece or goal events as out of bounds. It also permits brief “disruption” possession by the defending player without changing the overall phase. These are provider-derived rules, not independent human truth.

In the first 146 tracking frames, the initial home phase covers frames 29–122 under the provisional half-open reading. Tracking `possession.group` is null at frame 29 and says `away team` on **15** frames while the phase says home, including a visible 88–100 run before home possession resumes at 101. This may reflect a short disruption or a difference between player control and team phase; the sample does not establish which. Null `possession.group` occurs on 20 of the 136 sampled period-1 frames. Phase absence, null possession, and `ball_data.is_detected=false` are distinct conditions.

**Proposed conservative diagnostic mapping, not a full benchmark target:**

| Label | Evidence required | Current limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H | Valid tracking `period`; metadata `home_team.id` matches phase `team_in_possession_id`; `possession.group='home team'`; no boundary crossing or conflict. | Provider-consensus **proxy**, not independent truth. Keep detected/extrapolated ball as a separate quality flag. |
| A | Same checks against `away_team.id` and `possession.group='away team'`. | Same proxy limitation. |
| D | An affirmative, temporally bounded provider out-of-play/whistle/restart interval with checked alignment. | **Not established in this sample.** `game_interruption_before/after` names an interruption but lacks an exact D span. No-phase gaps alone are insufficient. |
| X | Null or non-period frames, null/ambiguous possession, provider disagreement, phase gaps, unverified stoppages, and boundary-crossing parts of phases. | These holes break official scorer runs; sparse H/A labels cannot support a meaningful whole-match spellF1. |

Classification: `possession.group` and phases are **provider-derived labels**; any conversion using event interruptions is an **event-derived proxy**; filling phase gaps with D would be a **heuristic pseudo-label**. No independently annotated H/A/D truth was verified. A full-frame H/A/D/X file with authoritative D and transition boundaries cannot yet be derived. Official scorer output on such provisional labels would measure agreement with a constructed target, and a sparse consensus target would make spellF1 especially misleading.

## 10 fps adaptation feasibility (proposal only)

The frozen source uses 25 fps defaults: 10 confirmation frames (0.4 s), 250 maximum-gap frames (10 s), three out-of-bounds frames (~0.12 s), five still frames (0.2 s), and a 4 m/s strike threshold. The scorer's spell-duration and transition-tolerance diagnostics also assume 25 fps.

| Option | Physical time and alignment | Restart, gap, touch risks | Complexity / decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Native 10 fps, explicit rate-scaled parameters** | Keep observed frame/timestamp grid, convert pitch-centred metres to the engine's coordinate contract, and predeclare a conservative ceiling rule: confirmation 4 frames, max gap 100, out confirmation 2, restart still 2. Source `ff686e3` remains unchanged, but the *configuration* is a named rate-adapted reference, not byte-identical 25 fps defaults. Match labels at 10 fps. | No ball-speed field is observed. Derive speed only from consecutive genuine ball observations in physical time; reject gaps and extrapolated jumps for strike evidence. Out confirmation becomes 0.2 s because 10 fps cannot represent 0.12 s exactly. | **Preferred for E1B ingestion feasibility**, conditional on label/license gates. Keep all transformations explicit. |
| B. Resample positions to 25 fps | Gives native engine defaults and scorer duration units, but inserts 1–2 synthetic frames between real observations. | Interpolation may create false velocity changes/touches, false still runs, and inflated consecutive-control evidence; zero-order hold has different biases. Missing-ball gaps and event times need exact masks. | More complex; defer unless a documented resampling protocol is tested. |
| C. Time-based wrapper | Could use real timestamps and preserve thresholds in seconds. | Must reproduce state-machine edge cases, restart detection, and missing-observation semantics without altering frozen code. | Highest implementation and parity risk; defer. |

A 10 fps algorithm with unscaled 25 fps frame-count defaults would demand 1.0 s rather than 0.4 s to confirm control and hold gaps for 25 s rather than 10 s. Comparing that output with models using physical-time windows would be invalid. The preferred A result must be identified as rate adapted; comparisons to the original 25 fps competition run are **not** accuracy comparisons. Evaluate every candidate on the same observed grid, inputs, match IDs, and audited label definition. Exclude provider `possession` and phases from predictor features whenever they construct the target. For official spellF1, first obtain reliable whole-interval H/A/D labels; otherwise report coverage-limited proxy agreement only.

## Split and E1B gate

After verifying which of the 20 public match directories actually contain usable data and consistent permissions, freeze an eligibility list and deterministic match-level assignment **before** inspecting target scores. A provisional split for 20 eligible matches is 12 train / 4 development / 4 sealed test, stratified for teams, dates and coverage without looking at outcomes; if only 10 qualify, 6 / 2 / 2. Use the identical held-out matches and labels for frozen Phase 3B, motion-aware variants, Viterbi and ML. Report per-match and paired aggregate results; test labels never guide parameter choice. Competitor published scores are not comparable unless match set, label definition, scorer version and settings match.

**Recommendation: CONDITIONAL GO for E1B adapter implementation.** Conditions: written or explicit data-file research/derivative rights; a bounded, source-pinned input specification; documented half clipping and time alignment; a chosen proxy-label policy with honest coverage; and no claim of official whole-match possession accuracy until D intervals and transitions are defensible. The immediate blockers are dataset-file licensing, phase/possession conflict, unverified D boundaries, and lack of a ball-speed field. No full adapter should be merged or used for model selection before those are resolved.
