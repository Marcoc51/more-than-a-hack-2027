# E1D-A — IDSSE provider labels, ball speed and scoring gate

Decision: **GO for E1D-B's one-match, development-only provider-agreement measurement under the protocol below.** This is not independent possession truth, competition-match accuracy, or evidence of generalization. No Phase 3B predictions or model scores were produced here. The original Figshare v1 provenance and file hashes are in [E1C-D](IDSSE_AUDIT_E1C_D.md); all match XML and local audit scripts remain ignored under `external_samples/idsse/`.

## Verified meaning and match observations

The [publisher's data descriptor](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04505-y) defines ball-frame `BallStatus=0` as inactive when play is interrupted by the referee (examples include a foul or substitution) and `1` as active. It defines `BallPossession=1/2` as home/away possession by the player in control. The [floodlight DFL parser](https://floodlight.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/floodlight/io/dfl.html) likewise exposes status as Dead/Alive codes, separately from possession. These are provider annotations, not an independent review of each frame or the competition's private ground truth.

For the provenance-verified development match `J03WOH`, both codes appear on all 137,214 ball frames. There are no active frames without a valid `1/2` ownership code, no unsupported values, and no missing ball `X/Y`. `BallPossession` continues to hold a team ID during every inactive interval and never changes strictly inside one. The provisional precedence remains: `BallStatus=0 → D`; `BallStatus=1` with possession `1/2 → H/A`; otherwise `X`. A zero-X result thus reflects complete **coded coverage** on this match, not independently verified certainty or proof that all contested-ball states were annotated correctly. Never fill X on another match from adjacent codes or model predictions.

`D` occupies 65,269 frames (47.57%, about 43.5 minutes) of the two tracked halves. The 991.72-second inter-half gap contains **no frames** and is not part of that share or a continuous D spell. There are 54 first-half and 48 second-half inactive runs; nine last more than a minute, one more than two minutes. All nine contain an event within the run or two seconds of it, including substitutions, video review, fouls, or restart events. These observations make a stoppage interpretation structurally plausible. They do not validate the exact first and last D frames. `BallStatus=0` is defensible as **provider-declared dead ball** for agreement scoring, but the private competition's D annotation convention may differ.

The active-to-inactive and inactive-to-active status changes total 54/53 in the first half and 47/47 in the second. The first half begins active and ends inactive; the second begins and ends inactive. H/A ownership can change directly during active play and remains sticky through inactivity. Use status precedence rather than treating the retained possession code as live control.

## Ball-speed quality

The publisher documents frame `S` in km/h and `D` as distance since the preceding frame in centimetres. The table compares `S/3.6`, `D/4` (cm over 0.04 s), and horizontal displacement from consecutive `X/Y` (metres over 0.04 s). All values are local aggregate diagnostics, not model results.

| Half and status | Median `S/3.6` | Median `X/Y` step speed | Median `D/4` | Median absolute `S`–`X/Y` gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| First, active | 6.34 m/s | 6.05 m/s | 6.05 m/s | 0.44 m/s |
| Second, active | 6.62 m/s | 6.33 m/s | 6.34 m/s | 0.46 m/s |
| First, inactive | 3.89 m/s | 0 | 0 | 3.89 m/s |
| Second, inactive | 5.52 m/s | 0 | 0 | 5.52 m/s |

Every inactive frame with a preceding same-half frame repeats its preceding `X/Y`; `D=0` there. The first frame of the second half is inactive and has no preceding same-half comparison. Yet 41,855 inactive frames have `S/3.6 > 4 m/s`. Active frames show substantially closer agreement among `S`, `D`, and coordinate displacement. This demonstrates that `S` can be stale or represent a differently processed quantity during inactive play; the exact production cause is unknown. Two normalized speed entries are intentionally NaN at the half starts, and no speed bridges a missing observation or half boundary. Coordinate precision and smoothing can explain some active-frame differences; no correction factor was fitted.

For the **current** adapter path, `predict` calls `game_evidence` with empty event/Smart Tagging lists. Inspection of the frozen engine shows ball speed is used there only to locate a strike inside a tag window; `control_evidence` uses player distances. Thus the stale inactive `S` cannot affect current no-event predictions or manufacture restart evidence. A synthetic regression varies `S` while keeping positions fixed and verifies prediction invariance and no restart creation. Keep the raw `S` conversion for contract fidelity, but prohibit speed-based restart detection with IDSSE events until an external-only policy is explicitly specified and tested. A conservative future candidate is displacement-derived speed from genuinely consecutive same-half `X/Y` (or `D/4` after cross-validation), with NaN over gaps; do not silently substitute it or tune a threshold in E1D-B.

## Event-time reliability

The [publisher's technical validation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04505-y) warns that separately produced event and tracking timestamps differ. Its reported unsynchronized timing difference is approximately −0.37 ± 1.82 seconds, with a maximum of 27.34 seconds; no automatic synchronization correction was applied to the release. Our local nearest-status-transition diagnostic is consistent with second-scale offsets: median absolute offsets are approximately 1.5 s for throw-ins, 1.6 s for goal kicks, 1.7 s for free kicks, and 2.9 s for kickoffs. These nearest-neighbor comparisons do **not** prove the matched status change is the event's true boundary. `FinalWhistle` and `Substitution` event times can be tens of seconds from the nearest status edge because they may occur within a stoppage. Do not shift timestamps to force agreement, treat event gaps as dead ball, or use these event times as exact restart or D boundaries. Events remain audit context only and never enter model features in E1D-B.

## Frozen E1D-B scoring protocol

1. Source and split: use only original Figshare v1 `J03WOH` and its verified metadata/positions, identified as a **development** match. Retain file IDs, byte sizes, publisher MD5s, local SHA-256s, adapter revision, original engine revision `ff686e3`, scorer revision and checksum in the local manifest. Do not tune on held-out IDSSE matches.
2. Input: adapt tracking at native 25 fps with unchanged Phase 3B defaults. Use the current `predict` call with **no IDSSE event bridge** and no provider `BallStatus`/`BallPossession` in features. Do not alter the speed policy or engine before this reference run. State plainly that ball speed is carried in `Tracking` but unused by the no-tag prediction path.
3. Labels: score only frames with validated half, frame number and timestamp, distinct home/away IDs, and explicit supported status/ownership codes. For this match all 137,214 frames qualify. If another file has invalid codes, missing frames, unverified identity or gaps, stop full-match scoring; mark supported but uncertain frames X only under a reviewed rule and report coverage. Never impute labels or silently repair a timeline.
4. Half boundary: score the two half-open spans independently in time. For the existing pinned scorer's `valid_runs` to keep spells apart, construct an **ungraded structural X separator** between the two halves in both truth and predictions: evaluation indices `[0, 69424)` and `[69425, 137215)`, with index `69424` equal to X. This separator is not an observed IDSSE frame or a labelled halftime duration. Preserve original `(GameSection, N, T)` keys separately in the local manifest; do not misreport the evaluation index as the provider frame ID.
5. Metrics: use the checksum-verified, unchanged official metric implementation with the declared separator. Report its spellF1 and macroF1 and the number of graded frames **only as agreement with DFL provider annotations on one external development match**. Report H/A/D coverage and, if desired, per-half diagnostics. A predicted X on an eligible frame remains a miss; a truth X is ungraded. If X occurs inside a half on a future match, the current comparison harness rejects it: do not fill it or report whole-match spellF1 until the coverage and temporal-run policy is reviewed.
6. Interpretation: no numeric comparison with the competition leaderboard, private match, or other methods on different files/settings. No claim of independent possession accuracy or superiority. One development match cannot establish generalization; later match-level testing must use the predeclared held-out IDs and identical labels/settings for all algorithms.

This protocol resolves the current one-match gate for an E1D-B **provider-agreement** measurement. Remaining risks are status boundary errors, sticky active ownership, event-time mismatch, inactive-period `S`, and the source's own processing conventions. The benchmark infrastructure may be reviewed for a separate merge into main **without** model changes or external data, but this audit does not authorize or recommend merging scoring claims into the competition submission before the E1D-B result and review.
